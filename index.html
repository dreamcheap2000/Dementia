Dementia Syndromes
Alzheimer's Disease & Major Neurocognitive Disorders

Epidemiology · Pathophysiology · Clinical Features · Biomarkers

Alzheimer's Disease (AD) Most Common · 60–80%

Epidemiology & Risk Factors

  • Prevalence: ~50 million globally (WHO 2023)
  • Age >65: greatest risk; doubles every 5 years
  • APOE ε4 allele: 3–4× risk (heterozygote), ~8–12× (homozygote)
  • Modifiable: HTN, DM, obesity, physical inactivity, hearing loss
  • Protective: education, Mediterranean diet, social engagement

Neuropathology (NIA-AA 2018)

  • Amyloid (A): Extracellular Aβ42 plaques; earliest biomarker
  • Tau (T): Hyperphosphorylated tau → NFTs; Braak stages I–VI
  • Neurodegeneration (N): Hippocampal atrophy, FDG-PET hypometabolism
  • Synapse loss correlates best with cognitive decline

Clinical Stages

  • Preclinical: Biomarker+ but cognitively normal
  • MCI due to AD: Objective memory impairment, intact ADL
  • Mild AD: Episodic memory, MMSE ~18–26
  • Moderate AD: Language, praxis, MMSE ~10–18
  • Severe AD: Mutism, incontinence, MMSE <10

Cognitive Signature

  • Anterograde episodic memory deficit (hallmark)
  • Hippocampal-dependent recall > recognition failure
  • Later: language (anomia), visuospatial, executive
  • Relative sparing: procedural memory, insight (early)

Key reference: Jack CR Jr et al., NIA-AA Research Framework. Alzheimers Dement. 2018;14(4):535–562.

Vascular Dementia (VaD) 2nd Most Common · 15–20%

Subtypes

  • Multi-infarct dementia: Cortical strokes, stepwise decline
  • Small vessel disease (SVD): Lacunar infarcts, WMH; gradual
  • Strategic infarct: Single infarct (thalamus, angular gyrus) → dementia
  • CADASIL: NOTCH3 mutation; migraine, strokes, younger onset
  • Mixed dementia: VaD + AD (~50% of dementia autopsy series)

Cognitive Signature

  • Executive dysfunction & attention: prominent early
  • Processing speed slowed
  • Memory: impaired encoding AND retrieval (unlike AD)
  • Motor signs: focal deficits, gait apraxia, pseudobulbar affect
  • Stepwise or abrupt onset

Diagnosis (VASCOG Criteria 2014)

  • Cognitive impairment + vascular brain injury (neuroimaging)
  • Temporal relationship OR predominant executive/attention deficit
  • MRI: WMH ≥25% of white matter OR ≥1 strategic infarct
  • Fazekas scale grades WMH severity

Management

  • Primary & secondary stroke prevention paramount
  • BP control (SBP <130 mm Hg target in SVD)
  • Antiplatelet therapy; statin; anticoagulation if AF
  • Cognitive rehab; exercise program

Ref: Sachdev P et al. Alzheimers Dement. 2014;10(5):539–550. VASCOG criteria.

Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD) 4 Clinical Variants

FTD encompasses a spectrum of disorders sharing TDP-43, tau, or FUS proteinopathy. It is the most common dementia under age 60. (Rascovsky K et al. Brain 2011;134:2456–2477)

① Behavioural Variant FTD (bvFTD)

  • Early personality change, disinhibition, apathy, loss of empathy
  • Compulsive behaviour, oral hyperphagia
  • Executive dysfunction > memory (initially)
  • Frontal/anterior temporal atrophy on MRI; FDG-PET hypometabolism
  • Genetics: C9orf72 (most common), MAPT, GRN

② Semantic Variant PPA (svPPA)

  • Progressive loss of word meaning and object recognition
  • Fluent speech with semantic errors and anomia
  • Surface dyslexia/dysgraphia
  • Asymmetric anterior temporal atrophy (often L > R)
  • TDP-43 type C pathology predominant

③ Non-fluent/Agrammatic Variant PPA (nfvPPA)

  • Effortful, halting speech; agrammatism
  • Motor speech difficulties (apraxia of speech)
  • Comprehension relatively preserved initially
  • Inferior frontal / left anterior insula atrophy
  • Tau pathology (CBD, PSP); some TDP-43

④ FTD-Motor Neuron Disease (FTD-MND)

  • bvFTD features + upper/lower MND signs
  • Prognosis poor: median survival ~2.5 years
  • TDP-43 type B; strongly associated with C9orf72
  • Rapidly progressive course
  • Bulbar symptoms common; dysphagia early

Lewy Body Dementias: DLB & PDD

Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB) DLB

McKeith IG et al. Neurology 2017;89:88–100 (4th Consensus Criteria)

  • Core features (≥2 = probable DLB):
  • ① Fluctuating cognition & attention
  • ② Recurrent visual hallucinations (well-formed, vivid)
  • ③ REM sleep behaviour disorder (RBD)
  • ④ Spontaneous parkinsonism
  • Supportive biomarkers: Low DaT-SPECT, 123I-MIBG scintigraphy, PSG-confirmed RBD
  • Cognitive signature: Attention, executive, visuospatial >> memory
  • 1-year rule: Cognitive symptoms precede or coincide with parkinsonism

Parkinson's Disease Dementia (PDD) PDD

Emre M et al. Lancet Neurol 2007;6:708–720

  • Well-established PD > 1 year before dementia onset
  • Same α-synuclein pathology as DLB
  • Cognitive signature: Subcortical-frontal pattern
  • Executive, attention, visuospatial; memory retrieval deficit
  • Hallucinations (often medication-induced); depression common
  • Treatment: Rivastigmine only ChEI with Level A evidence for PDD

⚠ DLB vs PDD: The "1-Year Rule"

Dementia before/within 1 yr of parkinsonism → DLB
Parkinsonism >1 yr before dementia → PDD

Critical Prescribing Warning: Antipsychotics (especially conventional) are contraindicated in DLB — severe neuroleptic sensitivity reactions (autonomic crisis, worsening parkinsonism) occur in ~50%.

Metabolic & Autoimmune Encephalopathy

Key principle: These are potentially reversible causes of dementia/cognitive impairment. Early recognition is critical. (Graus F et al. Lancet Neurol. 2016;15:391–404)

Metabolic Causes

  • Hypothyroidism: TSH elevated; "myxedema madness"; treat with levothyroxine
  • B12/folate deficiency: Subacute combined degeneration; macrocytic anaemia
  • Thiamine (B1) deficiency: Wernicke-Korsakoff; ophthalmoplegia, ataxia
  • Hepatic/renal encephalopathy: Asterixis, EEG triphasic waves
  • Hypercalcaemia, hyponatraemia
  • Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH): Triad: gait, incontinence, dementia; LP drainage test

Autoimmune Encephalitis

  • Anti-NMDAR encephalitis: Psychiatric prodrome → seizures → coma; oophoroma associated; IVIG/steroids/rituximab
  • LGI1 (VGKC complex): Limbic encephalitis; faciobrachial dystonic seizures; hyponatraemia
  • CASPR2: Limbic encephalitis, Morvan syndrome, peripheral nerve hyperexcitability
  • GAD65: Limbic encephalitis, cerebellar ataxia, stiff-person syndrome
  • Hashimoto encephalopathy: Steroid-responsive; elevated TPO antibodies
  • Paraneoplastic (Hu, Yo, Ri): Anti-tumour; poor prognosis; treat underlying malignancy

Non-Pharmacological Interventions & Hospice/Palliative Care

Evidence-Based Non-Pharmacological

  • Cognitive stimulation therapy (CST): Level 1A evidence; improves cognition & QoL (Spector A et al. Br J Psychiatry 2003)
  • Physical exercise: Aerobic + resistance training; slows cognitive decline
  • Music therapy: Reduces agitation and depression (Cochrane 2020)
  • Reminiscence therapy: Improves mood; caregiver interaction
  • Occupational therapy: Caregiver training; functional independence
  • Sleep hygiene & light therapy: Circadian regulation; reduces sundowning

Hospice Eligibility (FAST Stage ≥7A)

  • FAST ≥7A + ≥1 of: aspiration pneumonia, pyelonephritis/UTI with sepsis, septicemia, decubitus ulcer grade 3–4, fever recurring after antibiotics, inability to maintain sufficient fluid/calorie intake
  • Goals: comfort, dignity, symptom management
  • Comfort measures: Oral care, pressure ulcer prevention, dysphagia management
  • PEG tube: No mortality/comfort benefit in advanced dementia (Finucane TE et al. JAMA 1999) — consider hand feeding
  • ACP: POLST/MOLST, DNR, designated healthcare proxy; early in disease course

Caregiver Support

  • Caregiver burnout: screen with Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI)
  • Psychoeducation, respite care, support groups
  • Alzheimer's Association 24/7 helpline
  • Home safety assessment; driving cessation counseling

Dysphagia Management

  • FEES/VFSS swallowing evaluation
  • IDDSI texture modification (Levels 0–7)
  • Positioning: upright 90°, chin tuck
  • Aspiration pneumonia: most common cause of death in advanced AD
